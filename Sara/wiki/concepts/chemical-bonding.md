# Chemical Bonding

## Definition
The force of attraction between atoms that holds them together in molecules and compounds. Results from the tendency of atoms to achieve a stable electron configuration (usually octet/duplet).

## Why It Matters
Chemical bonding determines: molecular shape (→ physical properties, reactivity), polarity (→ intermolecular forces, solubility), bond strength (→ reaction enthalpies), and magnetic properties.

## Types of Bonds

**Ionic Bond:**
- Electron transfer from low-IE metal to high–ΔegH non-metal
- Strong electrostatic attraction; high MP/BP; conducts when dissolved/melted
- Strength measured by lattice enthalpy (NaCl = 788 kJ/mol)
- More covalent character when: cation is small + highly charged; anion is large (Fajan's rules)

**Covalent Bond:**
- Electron pair sharing between atoms
- Single=σ only; Double=σ+π; Triple=σ+2π
- σ bonds allow rotation; π bonds prevent it
- Bond strength: triple > double > single (for same atoms)
- Bond length: triple < double < single

**Coordinate/Dative Bond:**
- One atom provides both electrons of the shared pair (e.g., NH₄⁺, BF₃·NH₃)

**Metallic Bond:**
- Sea of delocalised electrons; explains conductivity and malleability

## Bond Polarity
- Electronegativity difference (ΔEN) → polar bond → dipole moment (μ)
- ΔEN = 0: nonpolar covalent; ΔEN large (>1.7 typically): ionic
- Net molecular dipole = vector sum of all bond dipoles
- CO₂: linear → zero net dipole; H₂O: bent → nonzero dipole

## Resonance
When a single Lewis structure is insufficient, the molecule is a hybrid of multiple resonance structures. Bond lengths in resonance hybrid are intermediate between single and double bonds.
- O₃: O–O bond length = 128 pm (between single 148 pm and double 121 pm)
- CO₃²⁻: all three C–O bonds equivalent
