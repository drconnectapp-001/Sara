# Mechanical Properties

## Definition

Mechanical properties describe how materials (solids and fluids) respond to applied forces — their elastic, plastic, and flow behaviour. For solids: stress, strain, and elastic moduli (Young's, Bulk, Shear). For fluids: pressure, buoyancy, viscosity, and surface tension.

## Why It Matters

Mechanical properties contribute 2–3 JEE questions per paper — Bernoulli/continuity is almost guaranteed, Young's modulus numericals appear frequently. Understanding these properties bridges microscopic intermolecular forces to macroscopic engineering-scale behaviour.

## Evidence & Examples

**Solids (Elastic Moduli):**
- Young's Modulus: Y = (F·L)/(A·ΔL) [Pa]; longitudinal stress/strain
- Bulk Modulus: B = –P/(ΔV/V) [Pa]; 1/B = compressibility
- Shear Modulus: G = (F/A)/(Δx/L); solids only
- Elastic PE in wire: U = ½ × stress × strain × volume = F²L/(2AY)

**Fluids — Key Results:**
- Pressure at depth: P = P₀ + ρgh
- Archimedes: F_buoy = ρ_liquid × V_submerged × g
- Continuity: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂
- Bernoulli: P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant
- Torricelli: efflux speed = √(2gh)
- Stokes: F_drag = 6πηrv; terminal velocity vₜ = 2r²(ρ–ρ_f)g/(9η)
- Surface tension: excess pressure in soap bubble = 4T/r; in liquid drop = 2T/r
- Capillary rise: h = 2T cosθ/(ρgr)
