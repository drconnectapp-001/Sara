# Organic Chemistry Basics

## Definition
The foundational electronic and structural framework for all organic chemistry: why carbon forms so many compounds, how to name them (IUPAC), and the electronic effects that control reactivity — inductive effect, resonance, hyperconjugation, and bond fission types.

## Why It Matters
Every organic reaction (alkanes, alkenes, alkyl halides, alcohols, carbonyl compounds) requires these concepts to explain WHY a product forms. JEE questions heavily test IUPAC naming of complex structures, electronic effect predictions, and carbocation/free radical stability orders.

## Carbon's Special Properties
- **Catenation**: C–C bonds are unusually stable → long chains, rings, branching
- **Tetravalence**: exactly 4 bonds; type determines geometry:
  - sp³ → tetrahedral (109.5°); sp² → trigonal planar (120°); sp → linear (180°)
- sp hybridised C is most electronegative (highest s-character); forms shortest, strongest C–H bonds

## Bond Fission Types
| Type | Mechanism | Fragments | Arrow notation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homolytic | one electron to each atom | free radicals (·) | half-headed (fishhook) arrows |
| Heterolytic | both electrons to one atom | carbocation (C⁺) or carbanion (C⁻) | full-headed curved arrows |

**Carbocation stability**: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃⁺ (stabilised by alkyl inductive donation + hyperconjugation)
**Carbanion stability**: opposite — 3° < 2° < 1° (alkyl groups destabilise negative charge)
**Free radical stability**: 3° > 2° > 1° (same pattern as carbocations)

## Electronic Displacement Effects

### Inductive Effect (I)
σ-bond polarisation transmitted along the chain; drops off rapidly (negligible beyond 3 bonds)
- **−I groups** (electron-withdrawing): –NO₂, –CN, –COOH, halogens (F > Cl > Br > I)
- **+I groups** (electron-donating): alkyl groups (tert > sec > primary > methyl — more alkyl = more +I)

### Resonance (Mesomeric) Effect (R)
Delocalization through conjugated π system or lone pair overlap
- **+R**: lone pair donors into ring/π system: –NH₂, –OH, –OR, halogens (ortho/para directors)
- **−R**: electron acceptors from π system: –NO₂, –CN, –CHO, –COOH (meta directors, deactivating)
- More canonical structures + more equivalent energies → greater resonance stabilisation

### Electromeric Effect (E)
Temporary π-electron shift toward attacking reagent (only when reagent approaches)
- +E: shift away from C=O (electrophile attacks O side)
- −E: shift toward C=O

### Hyperconjugation
Delocalization of σ C–H bond electrons of adjacent alkyl group into empty p-orbital or π-system
- Often called "no-bond resonance"; explains why more α-H → more hyperconjugation → more stabilisation
- Explains +I character of alkyl groups and greater stability of more substituted carbocations and alkenes

## IUPAC Nomenclature Rules
1. Longest chain containing the principal functional group = parent chain
2. Number to give principal functional group the lowest locant
3. **Priority order** (highest = principal group, must be suffix):
   –COOH > –SO₃H > –COOR > –COCl > –CONH₂ > –CN > –CHO > C=O > –OH > –NH₂ > C=C > C≡C
4. Halogens, nitro, alkoxy always as prefixes only
5. Substituents listed alphabetically; di/tri/tetra/iso/neo considered in alphabetical order; sec/tert NOT considered
6. Benzene: ortho (1,2), meta (1,3), para (1,4); phenyl = –C₆H₅

## Nucleophiles vs Electrophiles
- **Nucleophile**: electron-pair donor; attacks electron-deficient (δ+) centres; e.g., OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃, H₂O, R⁻
- **Electrophile**: electron-pair acceptor; attacks electron-rich centres; e.g., H⁺, Cl⁺, NO₂⁺, R⁺, BF₃, AlCl₃
