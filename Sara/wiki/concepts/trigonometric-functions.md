# Trigonometric Functions

## Definition

Trigonometric functions (sin, cos, tan, cot, sec, cosec) are functions of an angle defined via the unit circle — sin x = y-coordinate and cos x = x-coordinate of the point at angle x on the unit circle. They are periodic, continuous, and form the basis of Fourier analysis and wave mathematics.

## Why It Matters

Trigonometry is tested in EVERY JEE paper (3–5 direct questions) and appears as a tool in physics (SHM, waves, projectile, circular motion), coordinate geometry, and calculus. Mastery of identities is non-negotiable.

## Evidence & Examples

**Reference Table:**
| θ | 0 | 30° | 45° | 60° | 90° |
|---|---|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| sin | 0 | 1/2 | 1/√2 | √3/2 | 1 |
| cos | 1 | √3/2 | 1/√2 | 1/2 | 0 |
| tan | 0 | 1/√3 | 1 | √3 | ∞ |

**Must-Know Identities:**
- sin²θ + cos²θ = 1; 1+tan²θ = sec²θ; 1+cot²θ = cosec²θ
- sin(A±B) = sinA cosB ± cosA sinB
- cos(A±B) = cosA cosB ∓ sinA sinB
- sin2A = 2sinA cosA; cos2A = cos²A–sin²A = 1–2sin²A = 2cos²A–1
- sin3A = 3sinA–4sin³A; cos3A = 4cos³A–3cosA
- Range of a sinθ + b cosθ: [–√(a²+b²), √(a²+b²)]

**General Solutions:**
- sinθ = sinα → θ = nπ + (–1)ⁿα
- cosθ = cosα → θ = 2nπ ± α
- tanθ = tanα → θ = nπ + α

**Sum-to-Product (MEMORISE):**
- sinC + sinD = 2 sin((C+D)/2) cos((C–D)/2)
- cosC + cosD = 2 cos((C+D)/2) cos((C–D)/2)
- cosC – cosD = –2 sin((C+D)/2) sin((C–D)/2)
