# Mole Concept

## Definition
A **mole** is the SI unit for amount of substance. One mole contains exactly **6.02214076 × 10²³** elementary entities (atoms, molecules, ions, electrons, etc.). This number is the **Avogadro constant (Nₐ)**.

Molar mass of a substance = atomic/molecular mass expressed in g/mol.

## Why It Matters
The mole is chemistry's conversion bridge between the microscopic (atoms) and macroscopic (grams, litres) worlds. Every stoichiometry calculation passes through the mole. You cannot solve concentration, yield, or limiting reagent problems without it.

## Key Formulas

| Quantity | Formula |
|---------|---------|
| Moles from mass | n = mass(g) / molar mass(g·mol⁻¹) |
| Moles from molecules | n = N / Nₐ |
| Moles of gas (STP) | n = V(L) / 22.4 |
| Molarity | M = n(solute) / V(solution in L) |
| Mole fraction | χₐ = nₐ / (nₐ + n_b + ...) |
| Mass % | (mass solute / mass solution) × 100 |

## Evidence & Examples
- 1 mol H₂O = 18 g = 6.022×10²³ molecules = 22.4 L at STP
- 1 mol NaCl = 58.5 g (Na: 23 + Cl: 35.5)
- Glucose C₆H₁₂O₆: empirical formula CH₂O (molar mass 30), molecular formula C₆H₁₂O₆ (molar mass 180)
- Limiting reagent: the reactant providing fewer moles relative to stoichiometric need
