# Periodic Trends

## Definition
Systematic variation in physical and chemical properties of elements as you move across a period (left→right) or down a group in the Periodic Table, explained by changes in nuclear charge, atomic radius, and electron shielding.

## Why It Matters
Periodic trends allow prediction of properties for any element without memorizing all 118 individually. They explain bond polarity (Ch 4), reactivity, oxidation states, and acid/base strength.

## Trend Summary Table

| Property | Across Period (→) | Down Group (↓) | Exceptions |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|-----------|
| Atomic radius | Decreases | Increases | — |
| Ionization enthalpy | Increases | Decreases | B < Be; O < N |
| Electron gain enthalpy | More negative | Less negative | F < Cl (size effect) |
| Electronegativity | Increases | Decreases | — |
| Metallic character | Decreases | Increases | — |

## Detailed Notes

**Atomic Radius:**
- Across period: same shell, increasing Z → stronger pull → smaller radius
- Down group: new shells added, shielding increases → radius grows
- Period 2 (pm): Li(152) > Be(111) > B(88) > C(77) > N(74) > O(66) > F(64)

**Ionization Enthalpy (IE):**
- IE₁ of B < Be: B's 2p electron easier to remove than Be's 2s (2p slightly higher energy, more shielded)
- IE₁ of O < N: N has half-filled 2p (extra stability); O has paired electron that repels more, easier to remove
- IE₂ of Na > IE₂ of Mg: Na's 2nd electron comes from inner shell (n=2) vs Mg's from n=3

**Electron Gain Enthalpy:**
- Group 17 most negative (want 1 more e⁻ to complete octet)
- F less negative than Cl despite higher electronegativity: F is tiny, added electron experiences strong repulsion from existing 2p electrons in compact space

**Electronegativity:**
- F = 4.0 (highest of all elements)
- Period 2: Li(1.0), Be(1.5), B(2.0), C(2.5), N(3.0), O(3.5), F(4.0)
- Noble gases not assigned (don't typically form bonds)

## Evidence & Examples
- Gallium (Ga) was predicted by Mendeleev as eka-aluminium before discovery — trend-based prediction works
- O₂ is paramagnetic (2 unpaired electrons in π* MOs) — electronic configuration confirms this
- HF has anomalously high boiling point (19.5°C vs –85°C for HCl) — strong intermolecular H-bonding due to F's high electronegativity
