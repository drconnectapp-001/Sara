# Chapter 3: Classification of Elements and Periodicity in Properties

**Source:** [[raw/Chemistry/Part-01/kech103.md]] | **Date ingested:** 2026-05-12 | **Type:** textbook-chapter (NCERT Class 11 Chemistry Part 1)

## Summary
History of periodic classification leading to the Modern Periodic Table. Key focus on explaining periodic trends (atomic radius, ionization enthalpy, electron gain enthalpy, electronegativity) in terms of electronic configuration and nuclear charge.

## Key Points

### Historical Development
- **Döbereiner's Triads (1829):** elements in groups of 3 where middle element's atomic mass ≈ average of other two (Li-Na-K; Ca-Sr-Ba; Cl-Br-I)
- **Newlands' Law of Octaves (1865):** every 8th element has similar properties to the first — failed beyond Ca
- **Lothar Meyer (1868):** periodic variation of physical properties with atomic weight
- **Mendeleev (1869):** arranged elements by increasing atomic weight; left gaps for undiscovered elements; predicted properties (eka-aluminium=Gallium; eka-silicon=Germanium — confirmed experimentally)
- **Mendeleev's anomalies:** atomic weight order violated for Ar/K, Co/Ni, Te/I — corrected by Modern Periodic Law
- **Modern Periodic Law:** properties of elements are periodic functions of their **atomic number** (Moseley, 1913)

### Modern Periodic Table Structure
- **18 groups, 7 periods**
- Period 1: 2 elements (1s filled); Period 2 & 3: 8 elements; Period 4 & 5: 18 elements; Period 6 & 7: 32 elements
- **s-block (Groups 1-2):** alkali metals (ns¹) + alkaline earth metals (ns²); reactive, low IE
- **p-block (Groups 13-18):** ns²np¹⁻⁶; metals, non-metals, metalloids
- **d-block (Groups 3-12):** transition metals; (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻²; variable oxidation states, paramagnetism, catalytic activity
- **f-block (lanthanoids 4f, actinoids 5f):** inner transition elements; placed separately

### Periodic Trends

**Atomic Radius:**
- Decreases across a period (increasing nuclear charge pulls electrons closer, same shell)
- Increases down a group (new shell added; shielding increases)
- Period 2 values (pm): Li(152) > Be(111) > B(88) > C(77) > N(74) > O(66) > F(64)
- Group 1 values (pm): Li(152) < Na(186) < K(231) < Rb(244) < Cs(262)

**Ionization Enthalpy (ΔiH):**
- Energy required to remove outermost electron from gaseous atom in ground state
- Increases across period (generally); decreases down group
- **Exceptions:** IE₁(Be) > IE₁(B) — 2p electron more easily removed than 2s; IE₁(N) > IE₁(O) — half-filled 2p is stable (paired electron in O easier to remove)
- Na has lower IE₁ than Mg but higher IE₂ than Mg (Mg's 2nd electron from same shell; Na's from inner shell)

**Electron Gain Enthalpy (ΔegH):**
- Energy change when electron added to gaseous atom
- Most negative for Group 17 (halogens): F(–328), Cl(–349), Br(–325), I(–295) kJ/mol
- Noble gases (Group 18): positive ΔegH (stable configuration)
- **Exception:** F has lower –ΔegH than Cl (small size, electron-electron repulsion in compact 2p orbital)

**Electronegativity:**
- Relative tendency to attract shared electrons
- Increases across period; decreases down group
- Fluorine has highest electronegativity (4.0 on Pauling scale)
- Period 2: Li(1.0) < Be(1.5) < B(2.0) < C(2.5) < N(3.0) < O(3.5) < F(4.0)
- Related to: non-metallic character (direct), atomic radius (inverse), metallic character (inverse)

**Valence:** equals group number for s and p blocks (or 8 minus group number)

## My Take
Periodic trends are tested both as standalone MCQs ("which has highest IE?") and as reasoning questions for bonding and reactivity in later chapters. The two trend exceptions — B<Be in ionization enthalpy and O<N — are extremely frequent MCQ distractors. The connection between electronegativity → bond polarity → dipole moment (Chapter 4) and between IE → ionic bond formation tendency (Chapter 4) makes this chapter foundational to everything that follows.
