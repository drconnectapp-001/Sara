# Chapter 4: Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure

**Source:** [[raw/Chemistry/Part-01/kech104.md]] | **Date ingested:** 2026-05-12 | **Type:** textbook-chapter (NCERT Class 11 Chemistry Part 1)

## Summary
Explains why and how atoms bond, covering ionic bonds, covalent bonds, VSEPR theory (geometry prediction), valence bond theory, hybridization, molecular orbital theory, and hydrogen bonding. Bridges electronic configuration (Ch 2) with the shapes and properties of actual molecules.

## Key Points

### Kössel-Lewis Approach
- **Octet rule:** atoms tend to achieve 8 electrons in valence shell (like noble gases)
- **Lewis symbols:** dots represent valence electrons around element symbol
- **Ionic bond (electrovalent):** electron transfer from metal to non-metal; electrostatic attraction between ions; favoured when low IE (metal) + high –ΔegH (non-metal); NaCl lattice enthalpy = –788 kJ/mol
- **Born-Haber cycle:** Hess's law applied to calculate lattice enthalpy via stepwise energy changes
- **Covalent bond:** electron pair sharing; Lewis dot structures show all bonding and lone pairs
- **Formal charge:** (valence e⁻) – (lone pair e⁻) – ½(bonding e⁻); minimizing formal charge gives most stable structure
- **Resonance:** when single Lewis structure insufficient (O₃, CO₃²⁻, CO₂); actual structure is hybrid of resonance forms; bond lengths intermediate between single and double
- **Octet rule exceptions:**
  - Incomplete octet: BF₃ (6 electrons on B), BeH₂ (4 electrons)
  - Odd-electron molecules: NO, NO₂
  - Extended octet: PCl₅ (10 e⁻), SF₆ (12 e⁻) — uses 3d orbitals (period 3+)

### Bond Parameters
- **Bond length:** distance between nuclei of bonded atoms; increases with atomic size; decreases with bond order (triple < double < single)
- **Bond enthalpy:** energy to break 1 mole of bond in gaseous state; stronger bond = higher enthalpy
- **Bond order:** number of bonds between two atoms; higher bond order → shorter length, higher enthalpy, greater stability
- **Polarity:** difference in electronegativity → dipole moment (μ = q×d); vector quantity; net dipole = vector sum of all bond dipoles
  - CO₂ = 0 (linear, cancels); H₂O ≠ 0 (bent); NH₃ ≠ 0; NF₃ ≠ 0 (but < NH₃)
- **Fajan's rules:** larger cation charge + smaller cation + larger anion → more covalent character in ionic bond

### VSEPR Theory (Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion)
- **Postulates:** electron pairs (bonding + lone) arrange to minimise repulsion; LP-LP > LP-BP > BP-BP repulsion
- **Shapes (no lone pairs):** 2=linear; 3=trigonal planar; 4=tetrahedral; 5=trigonal bipyramidal; 6=octahedral
- **Effect of lone pairs:** distort ideal geometry; reduce bond angles
  - CH₄: tetrahedral 109.5° → NH₃: pyramidal 107° → H₂O: bent 104.5°

### Valence Bond Theory
- Bond forms when half-filled orbitals overlap; electrons pair with opposite spins
- **σ bond:** head-on/axial overlap; symmetric about internuclear axis; free rotation
- **π bond:** lateral/sidewise overlap; not symmetric; no free rotation; weaker than σ
- Single bond = σ; Double bond = σ + π; Triple bond = σ + 2π

### Hybridization
- Mixing of atomic orbitals of similar energy → new equivalent hybrid orbitals
- **sp (linear, 180°):** BeH₂, BeCl₂, C₂H₂ (each C); 2 σ bonds + 2 π bonds in C₂H₂
- **sp² (trigonal planar, 120°):** BF₃, BCl₃, C₂H₄ (each C), SO₃
- **sp³ (tetrahedral, 109.5°):** CH₄, NH₃, H₂O, CCl₄ (lone pairs distort angle)
- **sp³d (trigonal bipyramidal):** PCl₅, PF₅
- **sp³d² (octahedral):** SF₆, [CrF₆]³⁻
- **dsp² (square planar):** [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻, [Pt(Cl)₄]²⁻

### Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT)
- **LCAO:** MOs formed by linear combination of AOs (ψMO = ψA ± ψB)
- **Bonding MO:** ψ = ψA + ψB; lower energy; electrons stabilise molecule
- **Antibonding MO (*):** ψ* = ψA – ψB; higher energy; electrons destabilise
- **Bond order = ½(Nb – Na)**; positive BO = stable; 0 or negative = unstable
- **Conditions for AO combination:** similar energy; same symmetry about molecular axis; significant overlap
- **Energy order for Li₂ to N₂:** σ1s < σ*1s < σ2s < σ*2s < π2px = π2py < σ2pz < π*2px = π*2py < σ*2pz
- **Energy order for O₂, F₂:** σ1s < σ*1s < σ2s < σ*2s < σ2pz < π2px = π2py < π*2px = π*2py < σ*2pz
- Key results: H₂ (BO=1, diamagnetic); O₂ (BO=2, **paramagnetic** — 2 unpaired e⁻ in π*); N₂ (BO=3, diamagnetic); He₂ (BO=0, doesn't exist)

### Hydrogen Bonding
- **Condition:** H bonded to highly electronegative atom (F, O, N) → δ+H···X⁻
- **Intermolecular:** between different molecules; increases BP/MP; responsible for high BP of H₂O vs H₂S
- **Intramolecular:** within same molecule (e.g., o-nitrophenol, salicylaldehyde); lower BP than intermolecular isomers

## My Take
The single most concept-dense chapter in Class 11 chemistry. VSEPR shapes and their derivation are direct JEE questions. Hybridization must be applied — students often memorize examples without understanding how to determine it for a new molecule (count: σ bonds + lone pairs). MOT and bond order calculation is a reliable 1-2 mark question. The paramagnetic nature of O₂ (explained by MOT, not VBT) is a classic MCQ. Hydrogen bonding explains anomalous physical properties — boiling points of HF, H₂O, NH₃ relative to their group analogues.
