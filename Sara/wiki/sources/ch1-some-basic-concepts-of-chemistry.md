# Chapter 1: Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

**Source:** [[raw/Chemistry/Part-01/kech101.md]] | **Date ingested:** 2026-05-12 | **Type:** textbook-chapter (NCERT Class 11 Chemistry Part 1)

## Summary
Foundational chapter establishing the language of chemistry: SI units, significant figures, laws of chemical combination, Dalton's atomic theory, and the mole concept. Core quantitative tool is stoichiometry — balancing equations to find amounts of reactants and products.

## Key Points

- **Matter classification:** pure substances (elements, compounds) vs mixtures (homogeneous/heterogeneous)
- **SI base units (7):** metre (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K), mole (mol), candela (cd)
- **Temperature conversions:** K = °C + 273.15; °F = (9/5)°C + 32
- **Significant figures rules:** all non-zero digits count; zeros between non-zero count; trailing zeros after decimal count
- **Scientific notation:** maintains significant figures clearly (1×10² = 1 sig fig; 1.00×10² = 3 sig figs)
- **Laws of chemical combination:**
  - Law of Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier): mass of reactants = mass of products
  - Law of Definite Proportions (Proust): compound always has same element ratio by mass
  - Law of Multiple Proportions (Dalton): when two elements form multiple compounds, ratios of masses are simple whole numbers
  - Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes: gases react in simple volume ratios at same T, P
  - Avogadro's Law: equal volumes of gases at same T, P contain equal number of molecules
- **Dalton's Atomic Theory (1808):** atoms are indivisible, indestructible; atoms of same element are identical; compounds formed by combination of atoms in fixed ratio
- **Atomic mass unit (amu/u):** 1/12 mass of C-12 atom; 1 amu = 1.66056 × 10⁻²⁴ g
- **Molecular mass:** sum of atomic masses of all atoms in molecule
- **Formula mass:** used for ionic compounds (e.g., NaCl = 58.5 u)
- **Mole:** 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ entities (Avogadro's number, Nₐ); molar mass in g/mol numerically equals atomic/molecular mass in u
- **Empirical formula:** simplest whole-number ratio of atoms (e.g., CH₂O for glucose)
- **Molecular formula:** actual number of atoms (e.g., C₆H₁₂O₆ for glucose)
- **Limiting reagent:** reactant consumed first; limits amount of product formed
- **Concentration units:**
  - Mass percent (w/w%): (mass of solute/mass of solution) × 100
  - Molarity (M): moles of solute / litres of solution
  - Molality (m): moles of solute / kg of solvent
  - Mole fraction (χ): moles of component / total moles

Limiting reagent problems are frequently tested numerically. Concentration units (especially molarity) bridge this chapter directly to equilibrium (Ch 6).
