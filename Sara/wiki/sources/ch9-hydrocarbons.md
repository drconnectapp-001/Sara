# Ch 9 — Hydrocarbons

**Source:** [[raw/Chemistry/Part-02/kech203]] | **Date ingested:** 2026-05-12 | **Type:** book-chapter (NCERT Class 11 Chemistry Part 2, Unit 9)

## Summary
Complete treatment of hydrocarbons — alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, and aromatic (benzene). For each class: structure, nomenclature, isomerism, preparation methods, physical and chemical properties with mechanisms. The chapter is mechanistically rich: free radical, electrophilic addition, and electrophilic substitution mechanisms are all introduced here.

## Key Points

### Alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ — saturated)
- sp³ carbon, tetrahedral; C–C 154 pm, C–H 112 pm; bond angles 109.5°
- IUPAC: longest chain, lowest locants; suffix '-ane'; substituents alphabetical
- C1–C4 gases, C5–C17 liquids, C18+ solids; non-polar (van der Waals); branching ↓ bp (smaller surface area)
- **Conformations of ethane**: eclipsed (torsional strain maximum, least stable) vs staggered (least torsional strain, most stable); energy difference 12.5 kJ mol⁻¹; Sawhorse and Newman projections; rotation about C–C almost free at room temperature
- **Chemical reactions**:
  - Halogenation: free radical chain mechanism (hν); F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂; reactivity of H: 3° > 2° > 1°; mechanism: initiation (Cl–Cl homolysis), propagation, termination
  - Combustion: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O; incomplete → carbon black
  - Controlled oxidation: CH₄ + O₂ → CH₃OH (Cu, 523K, 100 atm); CH₄ + O₂ → HCHO (Mo₂O₃); ethane → acetic acid
  - Isomerisation: n-alkane + anhy. AlCl₃/HCl → branched alkane
  - Aromatization: n-C₆+ → benzene + H₂ (773K, V/Mo/Cr oxide on Al₂O₃)
  - Steam reforming: CH₄ + H₂O → CO + 3H₂ (Ni, 1273K) — industrial H₂ source
  - Pyrolysis/cracking: higher → lower alkanes + alkenes (free radical)
- **Preparation**: from unsaturated HC + H₂ (Pt/Pd/Ni); alkyl halide + Zn/H⁺; Wurtz reaction (RX + 2Na + XR → R–R); decarboxylation of sodium carboxylate + NaOH/CaO; Kolbe's electrolysis

### Alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ — one double bond)
- C=C: one σ (sp²–sp², 397 kJ mol⁻¹) + one π (p–p lateral, 284 kJ mol⁻¹); C=C 134 pm; planar geometry around C=C (120°)
- π bond restricts rotation → geometric (cis/trans) isomerism; condition: each doubly bonded C must carry two different substituents
- cis more polar than trans (e.g., cis-but-2-ene μ = 0.33 D vs trans μ ≈ 0); trans has higher mp
- **Preparation**: from alkynes (Lindlar's catalyst → cis; Na/liq. NH₃ → trans); dehydrohalogenation of alkyl halides (alc. KOH, β-elimination; rate: I > Br > Cl; 3° > 2° > 1°); dehalogenation of vicinal dihalides (Zn); acid dehydration of alcohols (conc. H₂SO₄, β-elimination)
- **Chemical reactions**:
  - Addition of H₂ (Pt/Pd/Ni) → alkanes
  - Addition of X₂ (Br₂/CCl₄, reddish orange → colourless; test for unsaturation)
  - Addition of HX: **Markovnikov's rule** — H⁺ adds to C with more H; Br⁻ to C with fewer H (more substituted C gets X); basis: 3° carbocation > 2° > 1° > CH₃⁺
  - Anti-Markovnikov addition (peroxide effect/Kharasch effect): in presence of peroxides, HBr adds opposite (free radical mechanism); only HBr, not HCl or HI
  - Addition of H₂O (acid-catalysed): Markovnikov product (industrial alcohol production)
  - Oxidation: KMnO₄ (cold, dilute, alkaline) → diol (Baeyer's reagent test for unsaturation, purple → colourless); KMnO₄ (hot, conc.) → ketones/carboxylic acids/CO₂; O₃/Zn-H₂O (ozonolysis) → aldehydes/ketones (identifies position of double bond)
  - Polymerisation: n CH₂=CH₂ → (–CH₂–CH₂–)ₙ (polyethylene)

### Alkynes (CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ — one triple bond)
- C≡C: one σ + two π bonds; sp hybridised; linear (180°); C≡C 120 pm
- **Preparation**: from dihalides with alc. KOH (vicinal or gem); calcium carbide + H₂O → acetylene (CaC₂ + H₂O → HC≡CH + Ca(OH)₂)
- **Acidic character**: sp C–H significantly acidic; terminal alkynes react with Na, NaNH₂ (sodamide), AgNO₃/NH₃ (white ppt AgC≡CR), Cu(NH₃)₂Cl (red ppt) — used to distinguish from alkenes
- **Chemical reactions**:
  - Addition of H₂: Lindlar's catalyst → cis alkene; Na/liq. NH₃ → trans alkene; excess H₂/Ni → alkane
  - Addition of X₂: stepwise → dihalide then tetrahalide
  - Addition of HX: Markovnikov; vinyl halide; second HX → gem dihalide
  - Hydration: H₂O + H₂SO₄/HgSO₄ → enol → tautomerises to carbonyl (HC≡CH → CH₃CHO; others → ketone)
  - Polymerisation: 3 HC≡CH → benzene (at 873K over Cu/activated charcoal)

### Benzene and Aromaticity
- Resonance hybrid of two Kekulé structures; all C–C bonds 139 pm (between single 154 pm and double 134 pm)
- **Hückel's rule**: aromatic if planar, fully conjugated, 4n+2 π electrons (n = 0, 1, 2…); benzene has 6 π electrons (n=1) ✓
- **Electrophilic Substitution** (preferred over addition — to retain aromaticity):
  - Halogenation: X₂ + Lewis acid (FeBr₃, AlCl₃) → halobenzene + HX; mechanism: Br⁺ electrophile → arenium ion (σ complex) → deprotonation
  - Nitration: conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄ → nitrobenzene; electrophile = NO₂⁺ (nitronium ion)
  - Sulphonation: conc. H₂SO₄ (oleum) → benzenesulphonic acid; reversible
  - Friedel-Crafts alkylation: RX + AlCl₃ → alkylbenzene; electrophile = R⁺ carbocation
  - Friedel-Crafts acylation: RCOCl + AlCl₃ → acyl benzene; electrophile = RCO⁺ acylium ion
- **Directive influence of substituents**:
  - Ortho/para directors (activating): –OH, –NH₂, –NHR, –OR, halogens (deactivating but o/p); donate electrons via +R
  - Meta directors (deactivating): –NO₂, –CN, –CHO, –COOH, –SO₃H; withdraw electrons via −R
  - Halogens: deactivating (−I) but o/p directors (+R outweighs −I for position selectivity)
- Combustion of benzene: sooty flame (high C:H ratio)
- Carcinogenicity: benzene and polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons (PAH) are carcinogenic; benzo[a]pyrene (in cigarette smoke) is particularly dangerous

## My Take
This is one of the highest JEE-weightage chapters. The key exam areas: Markovnikov/anti-Markovnikov with mechanism justification, cis/trans conditions, ozonolysis product identification, EAS directive effects with reasons, Hückel aromaticity, and conformations (Newman/Sawhorse). The free radical halogenation mechanism of alkanes and the EAS mechanism of benzene are both written-answer staples. Staggered vs eclipsed conformations appear as concept questions. Preparation of alkynes via calcium carbide and acidic character tests (AgNO₃ ppt) are frequently tested.
