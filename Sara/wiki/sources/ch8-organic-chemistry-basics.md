# Ch 8 — Organic Chemistry: Some Basic Principles and Techniques

**Source:** [[raw/Chemistry/Part-02/kech202]] | **Date ingested:** 2026-05-12 | **Type:** book-chapter (NCERT Class 11 Chemistry Part 2, Unit 8)

## Summary
Foundational chapter for all of organic chemistry. Covers why carbon forms so many compounds (tetravalence, catenation), how to represent and name organic structures (IUPAC system), how to classify compounds by functional group, and the electronic mechanisms that drive organic reactions — inductive effect, resonance, electromeric effect, hyperconjugation, and bond fission types.

## Key Points

### Carbon's Uniqueness
- **Catenation**: carbon forms stable C–C bonds with itself, enabling chains, branches, rings
- **Tetravalence**: four bonds possible — sp³ (tetrahedral, 109.5°), sp² (trigonal planar, 120°), sp (linear, 180°)
- sp hybrid has highest s-character → shortest and strongest bonds, most electronegative carbon
- σ bonds: head-on overlap; π bonds: lateral overlap of parallel p orbitals — π bonds restrict rotation (basis of geometric isomerism)

### Structural Representations
- Lewis/dash structure → condensed formula → bond-line (skeletal) formula
- 3D: solid-wedge (toward viewer), dashed-wedge (away from viewer), normal line (in plane)
- Molecular models: framework, ball-and-stick, space-filling

### Classification of Organic Compounds
- Open chain (aliphatic) vs cyclic (alicyclic, aromatic, heterocyclic)
- Functional groups define chemical reactivity: –OH, –CHO, –COOH, C=C, C≡C, –X, –NH₂, –CN, C=O
- **Homologous series**: successive members differ by –CH₂ unit; share same general formula and functional group

### IUPAC Nomenclature
- Parent chain = longest carbon chain containing the principal functional group
- Numbering: lowest locant to principal functional group, then substituents
- Priority order (decreasing): –COOH > –SO₃H > –COOR > –COCl > –CONH₂ > –CN > –CHO > C=O > –OH > –NH₂ > C=C > C≡C; halogens/nitro/alkoxy always prefix
- Substituents listed alphabetically; prefixes di/tri/tetra/iso/neo are considered; sec/tert not considered for alphabetical ordering
- Benzene nomenclature: substituent prefix + benzene; ortho (1,2), meta (1,3), para (1,4) for disubstituted; phenyl group = –C₆H₅

### Isomerism
- **Structural**: chain, position, functional group, metamerism (different alkyl groups on either side of functional group)
- **Stereoisomerism**: geometrical (cis/trans, restricted rotation due to C=C or ring) and optical

### Reaction Mechanism — Bond Fission
- **Heterolytic cleavage**: shared pair goes to one atom → carbocation (C⁺, sp², trigonal planar, sextet) or carbanion (C⁻, sp³, distorted tetrahedral)
  - Carbocation stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃⁺ (alkyl groups stabilise via inductive + hyperconjugation)
  - Carbanion stability: 3° < 2° < 1° (opposite)
- **Homolytic cleavage**: one electron to each fragment → free radicals (half-headed arrow); 3° > 2° > 1° radical stability
- Ionic/heteropolar reactions vs free radical/homolytic reactions

### Electronic Displacement Effects
- **Inductive effect (I)**: σ-bond polarisation transmitted along chain; decreases rapidly (negligible after 3 bonds); –I groups: –NO₂, –CN, –COOH, halogens; +I groups: alkyl groups
- **Electromeric effect (E)**: temporary displacement of π electrons toward attacking reagent; +E = electrons away from C=O toward C; −E = toward C=O
- **Resonance (Mesomeric) effect**: delocalization via π system or lone pair conjugation; +R = electron-donating (–NH₂, –OH, halogens); −R = electron-withdrawing (–NO₂, –CN, –CHO, –COOH)
  - Resonance energy = difference between actual hybrid energy and most stable canonical form
  - More resonance structures + equivalent energies = greater stabilisation
- **Hyperconjugation**: delocalisation of σ-bond (C–H) electrons of alkyl group into adjacent empty/π orbital; no-bond resonance; explains alkyl group +I and radical/carbocation stabilisation

### Nucleophiles and Electrophiles
- **Nucleophile**: electron-pair donor; attacks electron-deficient (δ+) centre; examples: OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃, H₂O, carbanions
- **Electrophile**: electron-pair acceptor; attacks electron-rich centre; examples: carbocations, BF₃, AlCl₃, H⁺, Cl⁺

This is the most conceptually dense chapter in Part 2 — everything in organic chemistry (alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, halides, alcohols, aldehydes) rests on these foundations. JEE focuses heavily on: IUPAC naming of complex branched/functional-group compounds, identifying electrophiles/nucleophiles, predicting inductive + resonance effects on acidity/basicity/reactivity, and carbocation stability order.
