# Ch 7 — Redox Reactions

**Source:** [[raw/Chemistry/Part-02/kech201]] | **Date ingested:** 2026-05-12 | **Type:** book-chapter (NCERT Class 11 Chemistry Part 2, Unit 7)

## Summary
Redox reactions are reactions involving simultaneous oxidation and reduction. The chapter builds three progressive frameworks — classical (oxygen/hydrogen transfer), electronic (electron transfer), and oxidation number — and shows they are equivalent descriptions of the same phenomenon. Balancing methods and electrode processes bridge to electrochemistry.

## Key Points
- **Classical definition**: oxidation = addition of O / removal of H; reduction = removal of O / addition of H
- **Electronic definition**: oxidation = loss of electrons; reduction = gain of electrons. Oxidising agent = electron acceptor; reducing agent = electron donor
- **Oxidation number rules**: free element = 0; monoatomic ion = charge; O usually −2 (except peroxides −1, superoxides −½, OF₂ +2); H usually +1 (−1 in metal hydrides); F always −1; algebraic sum = 0 for neutral compound, = charge for ion
- **Types of redox reactions**:
  - Combination: A + B → C (at least one reactant elemental)
  - Decomposition: C → A + B (at least one product elemental)
  - Displacement: metal or non-metal displacement (activity/electrochemical series)
  - Disproportionation: element in intermediate oxidation state simultaneously oxidised and reduced (e.g., H₂O₂ → H₂O + O₂; Cl₂ + OH⁻ → ClO⁻ + Cl⁻)
- **Balancing methods**: (a) Oxidation number method — equalise increase and decrease of ON, balance H⁺/OH⁻/H₂O; (b) Half-reaction (ion-electron) method — balance each half separately, equalise electrons, add
- **Competitive electron transfer**: Zn > Cu > Ag in reducing power; halogen oxidising power F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂ — basis of electrochemical/activity series
- **Redox titrations**: MnO₄⁻ (self-indicator, pink end point); Cr₂O₇²⁻ uses diphenylamine; iodometric methods via starch-iodine blue colour
- **Standard electrode potential (E°)**: measured against SHE (H⁺/H₂ = 0.00 V); negative E° = stronger reducing agent; positive E° = weaker reducing agent; table from F₂/F⁻ (+2.87 V) to Li⁺/Li (−3.05 V)
- **Fractional oxidation states**: C₃O₂ (C = 4/3 avg), Br₃O₈ (Br = 16/3 avg), S₄O₆²⁻ (S = 2.5 avg) — reality shown by structures: element present in multiple whole-number states
- **Daniell cell**: Zn | ZnSO₄ || CuSO₄ | Cu; electrons flow Zn → Cu through external wire; ions migrate through salt bridge; introduces concept of electrode potential and redox couples (Zn²⁺/Zn, Cu²⁺/Cu)

## My Take
Redox is the most calculation-heavy chapter before electrochemistry. The three-tier framework (classical → electronic → ON) is pedagogically elegant. Key JEE areas: assigning oxidation numbers to complex ions/mixed oxides, identifying disproportionation reactions, balancing by half-reaction method in both acid and base, and using E° values to predict feasibility. The Standard Electrode Potential table (Table 7.1) is directly exam-relevant — memorise F₂, MnO₄⁻, Cr₂O₇²⁻, Cl₂, Cu²⁺, H⁺, Fe²⁺, Zn²⁺, Al³⁺, Li⁺ positions.
